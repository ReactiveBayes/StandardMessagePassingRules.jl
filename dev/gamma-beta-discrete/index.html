Gamma, Beta and discrete distributions

These nodes model positive quantities (the Gamma and inverse-Gamma nodes), probabilities (Beta, Dirichlet) and discrete outcomes (Bernoulli, Categorical, Poisson). Several of them form conjugate pairs: a Beta prior on a Bernoulli's probability, a Dirichlet on a Categorical's and a Gamma on a Poisson's rate.

In a conjugate pair, belief propagation is exact and in closed form. An observed outcome, a point mass, sends its parameter a message in the prior's family, such as a Beta towards a Bernoulli's p. The product of that message and the prior is the posterior. The rules towards a Gamma's shape and rate, and towards a Categorical's p, take marginals and are variational: they use expectations such as E[log out] under those marginals, so an uncertain out works as well as an observed one. Many nodes on this page serve as priors only. They have the message towards out and no rule towards their parameters, which a model then gives as constants. The coverage tables show which.

The nodes are the types of ExponentialFamily and Distributions, declared as nodes here, and all run under DefaultAlgorithm.

Example

An observed coin flip sends its bias a Beta likelihood. The message's log scale is log(1/2):

julia> using StandardMessagePassingRules, MessagePassingRulesBase, ExponentialFamily, BayesBase

julia> result = @call_message_update_rule(node = Bernoulli, target = :p, m = (out = PointMass(1.0),));

julia> getresult(result) isa Beta && all(params(getresult(result)) .≈ (2.0, 1.0))
true

julia> getlogscale(result) ≈ log(1 / 2)
true
